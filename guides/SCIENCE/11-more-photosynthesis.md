# More about photosynthesis

**Key words:** fertiliser · stomata (singular: stoma) · yield

## Chloroplasts and chlorophyll
- **Chlorophyll** captures energy from sunlight. It is kept inside **chloroplasts**, inside plant cells. So photosynthesis happens **inside chloroplasts**.
- Not all plant cells have chloroplasts, so not all of them can photosynthesise. In most plants the cells **in the leaves** have the most chloroplasts.
- **Plant cell (leaf):** cell wall · cell surface membrane · cytoplasm · vacuole · nucleus · **chloroplasts containing chlorophyll**.
- On warm sunny days plants make more carbohydrate than they need. They **store** some as **starch**, inside the chloroplasts. A way to check whether a leaf has been photosynthesising is to **test it for starch**.

## Think like a scientist — testing a leaf for starch (iodine test)
**You need:** a healthy plant kept in sunlight; burner, tripod and gauze; beaker (250 cm³); a large test tube; forceps; ethanol; iodine solution with a dropper; a tile.
:::warn
**Safety:** hot water — stand up while you work. **Turn off the flame before using ethanol** — ethanol is **very flammable**.
:::
:::steps
1. Heat water in the beaker until it **boils**.
2. Drop a leaf into the boiling water (**boiling breaks the cell membranes** so iodine can get in).
3. After about 2 minutes, **turn off the burner**.
4. Put some ethanol in the large test tube.
5. Move the leaf into the ethanol with forceps.
6. Stand the tube in the hot water — this safely heats the ethanol; the **green colour (chlorophyll) dissolves out** into the ethanol.
7. Take the leaf out (it is brittle) and dip it in warm water to soften it.
8. Spread it on a tile and add **iodine solution**. If there is **starch**, it turns **blue-black**.
:::

## Inside a leaf
| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Waxy layer** (on the upper surface) | Stops the leaf cells drying out |
| **Upper epidermis** | Protects the cells inside the leaf |
| **Palisade layer** | Tall cells with **lots of chloroplasts** — do **most** of the photosynthesis |
| **Spongy layer** | Cells with lots of **air spaces**; do a small amount of photosynthesis |
| **Vein** | Carries **water** to the cells in the leaf |
| **Lower epidermis** | Protects the cells inside the leaf |
| **Stoma** (plural **stomata**) | Tiny hole in the lower epidermis that lets **carbon dioxide** in from the air (and oxygen/water vapour out) |

- Chloroplasts are mostly in the cells of the **middle layers** of the leaf. Leaves are very **thin**, so sunlight easily reaches these cells.
- Water reaches the leaf cells through the **veins**. **Carbon dioxide diffuses** into the leaf through the **stomata** and then through the **air spaces** between the cells.

## Minerals and plant growth
- Farmers add **fertiliser** to make crops grow larger and produce a higher **yield** (yield = the quantity of crop the farmer harvests).
- Fertilisers contain **minerals**. Plants need only small quantities and get them from the soil through their **roots**. Two important minerals are **magnesium** and **nitrate**.

| Mineral | Why plants need it | If it is missing |
|---|---|---|
| **Magnesium** | Needed to make the green pigment **chlorophyll** | Leaves look **yellow** instead of green; the plant can't absorb much energy from sunlight, so it can't photosynthesise well or grow well |
| **Nitrate** (contains nitrogen atoms) | Needed to convert carbohydrates into **proteins** (for making new cells) and to make **chlorophyll** | Leaves die and the plant **stays small** |

- Farmers can **test the soil** to find which minerals are lacking. With **GPS** on their machinery, they can control how much fertiliser goes onto each part of the field.

## Think like a scientist — fertiliser and duckweed
Plan an experiment to find out how fertiliser affects the rate of growth of **duckweed** (tiny floating plants; measure growth by **counting leaves**, grow in Petri dishes).
- Decide on a **hypothesis**; choose the **independent variable** (e.g. fertiliser concentration — how many values?), the **dependent variable** (number of leaves — how many times?), the **variables to keep the same** (controlled), equipment, **risks** and a **prediction**.

:::key
**Variables (teacher's notes):**
- **Independent:** the factor that is **changed**.
- **Dependent:** the factor that is **measured**.
- **Controlled:** factors **always kept the same**.
:::
