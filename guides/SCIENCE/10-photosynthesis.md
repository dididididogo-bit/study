# Photosynthesis

**Key words:** chlorophyll · light intensity · photosynthesis

## How plants make food
- Plants make food by **photosynthesis**. *"Photo"* means **light**; *"synthesis"* means **making** → "making with light".
- In photosynthesis plants use:
  - **water**, which they get from the **soil**;
  - **carbon dioxide**, which they get from the **air**;
  - **energy**, which they get from **sunlight**.
- The energy in sunlight is captured by a green pigment called **chlorophyll**, inside some of the cells in the leaves.
- The plant uses this energy to make water and carbon dioxide **combine in a chemical reaction**. Two new substances are made: **glucose and oxygen**.

:::key
**Word equation for photosynthesis**
**water + carbon dioxide → glucose + oxygen** (energy from light, captured by chlorophyll)
- **Reactants:** water and carbon dioxide · **Products:** glucose and oxygen
- Photosynthesis happens in the **leaves**.
:::

## Why is photosynthesis important?
1. **It provides energy** (as chemical energy in nutrients) for most other organisms. Plants use sunlight to make glucose and other **carbohydrates**, which contain some of the energy that was in the sunlight. In a **food chain**, energy is passed from plants to animals: most of the energy in all the food in the world comes from plants.
2. **It provides oxygen** for the Earth's atmosphere, which animals and plants need for respiration. Oxygen is a **waste product** of photosynthesis; it is released from the leaves into the air. About **20 % of the air** is oxygen.

- When the Earth first formed (about **4.6 billion years ago**) there was almost **no oxygen** in the atmosphere. Oxygen began to collect when **bacteria** first began to photosynthesise. (Plants did not appear until about 4.7 million years ago, according to the book.) Without photosynthesis, animals could never have lived on Earth.

## Getting started — light vs. dark
Maize plants grown **in the light** are green, tall and healthy; plants grown **in the dark** are **pale/yellow**, weak and spindly, because they cannot photosynthesise (no light → no glucose, and no chlorophyll is made).
