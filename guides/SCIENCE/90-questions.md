# Questions

## 1.1 Photosynthesis
:::q 1.1 · p. 9 · Q1 · What are the reactants in photosynthesis?
?? **Water and carbon dioxide** (with energy from light).
:::
:::q 1.1 · p. 9 · Q2 · What are the products in photosynthesis?
?? **Glucose and oxygen.**
:::
:::q 1.1 · p. 14 · Q3 · A lizard eats a grasshopper; grasshoppers eat grass. Explain how the lizard relies on photosynthesis to provide it with energy.
?? The **grass** captures energy from sunlight by **photosynthesis** and stores it in carbohydrates. The **grasshopper** eats the grass and gets this energy, and the **lizard** eats the grasshopper. So the energy in the lizard's food originally came from photosynthesis.
:::
:::q 1.1 · p. 14 · Q4 · Explain one other reason (apart from food) why the lizard would not be able to survive if there were no plants on Earth.
?? There would be no **oxygen** in the air: oxygen is produced by photosynthesis, and the lizard needs oxygen for **respiration**.
:::

## 1.2 More about photosynthesis
:::q 1.2 · Getting started · Q1–3 · In which parts of a plant does photosynthesis happen? Why there, and why not in other parts?
?? Mainly in the **leaves** (and other green parts). They contain **chloroplasts with chlorophyll** and receive **light**. Roots are underground (no light) and have no chloroplasts, so they can't photosynthesise.
:::
:::q 1.2 · p. 18 · Starch test Q1a · In which part of a cell is starch stored?
?? In the **chloroplasts**.
:::
:::q 1.2 · p. 18 · Starch test Q1b · Explain why it was important to boil the leaf before the starch test could work.
?? Iodine solution can't get through **cell membranes**. Boiling **breaks the membranes apart**, so the iodine can reach the starch.
:::
:::q 1.2 · p. 18 · Starch test Q2 · Suggest why it is a good idea to remove the green colour from the leaf before adding iodine.
?? So the colour change (**blue-black**) is easy to see; the green chlorophyll would hide it.
:::
:::q 1.2 · p. 18 · Starch test Q3 · Did the leaf contain starch? If so, where did it come from?
?? **Yes** (if the plant was kept in sunlight). The starch was made from the **glucose produced by photosynthesis** and stored in the chloroplasts.
:::
:::q 1.2 · p. 18 · Starch test Q4 · If your leaf had green and white parts, which parts contained starch? Why?
?? Only the **green parts**. They contain **chlorophyll**, so they can photosynthesise and make glucose/starch. The white parts have no chlorophyll.
:::
:::q 1.2 · p. 20 · Q1 · Use what you know about diffusion to explain how carbon dioxide diffuses into the leaf.
?? Leaf cells **use up CO₂** in photosynthesis, so there is a **lower concentration** inside the leaf than in the air. CO₂ diffuses from **high to low concentration**: in through the **stomata**, through the **air spaces**, and into the cells.
:::
:::q 1.2 · p. 20 · Q2 · Leaf cells produce oxygen when they photosynthesise. Suggest what happens to this oxygen.
?? Some is used by the cells for **respiration**; the rest **diffuses out** of the leaf through the **stomata** into the air.
:::
:::q 1.2 · p. 21 · Q3 · Compare the way plants obtain protein with the way you and other animals obtain protein.
?? **Plants make** their own protein, using carbohydrates from photosynthesis plus **nitrate** absorbed from the soil. **Animals cannot** make protein from simple substances; they get it by **eating** plants or other animals.
:::
:::q 1.2 · p. 21 · Q4 · It can be difficult to tell a plant short of magnesium from one short of protein (nitrate). Suggest why.
?? Both cause **yellow leaves and poor growth**. Magnesium **and** nitrogen are both needed to make **chlorophyll**, so lacking either gives similar symptoms.
:::
:::q 1.2 · Activity 1.2.1 · Which surface of a leaf has most stomata? (leaf pushed under warm water)
?? Most bubbles come from the **lower surface** — that's where most **stomata** are. The bubbles contain **air (gases) from the air spaces** inside the leaf; warming makes the gases **expand**, so they come out through the stomata.
:::
:::q 1.2 · Duckweed · Part 2 Q1–2 · Did you change anything in your plan? Write a conclusion — does it support your hypothesis?
?? Personal answer from your own experiment. Example conclusion: "The more fertiliser added, the faster the duckweed grew (more leaves), up to a point. This **supports** my hypothesis."
:::

## 1.3 The carbon cycle
:::q 1.3 · p. 26 · Q1a · Draw a food chain with one plant and three animals.
?? Example: **grass → grasshopper → lizard → hawk**.
:::
:::q 1.3 · p. 26 · Q1b · The arrows show energy passing between organisms. Do they also show how carbon atoms pass from one organism to the next?
?? **Yes.** When an animal eats another organism, it takes in its **carbon compounds** (carbohydrates, proteins, fats), so carbon moves along the food chain in the same direction as the energy.
:::
:::q 1.3 · p. 26 · Q2 · Name three different compounds in your body that contain carbon atoms.
?? **Carbohydrates** (e.g. glucose), **proteins** and **fats**. (Also DNA, carbon dioxide.)
:::
:::q 1.3 · Indicator experiment · Q1–6 · Tubes A, B (water plant), C, D (maggots), E, F (plant + maggots); A, C, E in the dark.
?? **Expected results:**
- **A** (plant, dark): only respiration → CO₂ rises → **yellow**.
- **B** (plant, light): photosynthesis faster than respiration → CO₂ used up → **purple**.
- **C, D** (maggots, dark or light): respiration only → **yellow**.
- **E** (plant + maggots, dark): both respire → **yellow** (most CO₂).
- **F** (plant + maggots, light): plant's photosynthesis uses the CO₂ → **red/purple**.

Q2 — respiring: **all tubes** with organisms (A–F). Q3 — photosynthesising: **B and F** (plants in the light). Q4a — CO₂ used: **B and F**; Q4b — CO₂ given out: **A, C, D, E**. Q6 — platforms in every tube so **conditions are the same** (fair test — the platform is a controlled variable).
:::

## 1.4 Climate change
:::q 1.4 · Getting started · Match statements A–D to numbers 1–4 on the diagram.
?? Following the path of the energy: **B → 1** (energy from the Sun warms the ground), **D → 2** (some energy is reflected), **C → 4** (some reflected energy is blocked by CO₂ and stays close to Earth), **A → 3** (some passes out and is lost to space). *(Check against the numbers on your diagram.)*
:::
:::q 1.4 · p. 39 · Q1 · Describe what an asteroid is and where asteroids are found.
?? A **large rock** that orbits the Sun. Most are in the **asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter**.
:::
:::q 1.4 · p. 39 · Q2 · Outline three ways the asteroid collision 67 million years ago made it difficult for plants and animals to survive.
?? 1) **Dust** blocked sunlight, so plants couldn't **photosynthesise** → less food and disrupted **food chains**. 2) The Earth became **much colder**. 3) Huge **shock waves, fires/high temperatures and a tsunami** destroyed habitats near the impact.
:::
:::q 1.4 · p. 40 · Q3 · Look at the graph. How much higher was the mean temperature in 2016 than in 1880?
?? About **1 °C** higher (the graph reaches just over +1.0 °C in 2016).
:::

## 4.1 Plants and water
:::q 4.1 · Q1 · Describe how the structure of a root hair cell helps it to carry out its function.
?? It is **long and thin** and grows out between soil particles, giving a **very large surface area** for absorbing **water and mineral ions**. It has no chloroplasts (it is underground) and a large vacuole that takes in water.
:::
:::q 4.1 · Q2 · Why do plants need nitrate ions and magnesium ions?
?? **Nitrate** — to make **proteins** (for growth/new cells). **Magnesium** — to make **chlorophyll** for photosynthesis.
:::
:::q Worksheet 4.1A · Q1 · Which root hairs were better at absorbing water?
?? The **young** root hairs.
:::
:::q Worksheet 4.1A · Q2 · At what rate were the old root hairs absorbing water at 20 minutes?
?? About **1.7–2.0 mm³ per minute** (teacher's note: 1.7–2.0).
:::
:::q Worksheet 4.1A · Q3 · As time increases, the rate at which the young root hairs absorb water ___ and then ___.
?? **increases** … **stays the same**.
:::

## 4.2 Transpiration
:::q 4.2 · Getting started · Draw particles in liquid water and in water vapour; explain what happens when liquid water changes to a gas.
?? **Liquid:** particles close together, touching, moving around each other. **Gas:** particles far apart, moving fast in all directions. When water evaporates, particles gain energy and **escape from the liquid**, spreading out as a gas.
:::
:::q 4.2 · Q1 · Identify the upper epidermis, palisade layer and spongy layer on the diagram.
?? From top to bottom: **upper epidermis** (top row of flat cells) → **palisade layer** (tall cells packed with chloroplasts) → **spongy layer** (rounded cells with air spaces) → lower epidermis with stomata.
:::
:::q 4.2 · Q2 · Most transpiration happens from the underside of a leaf. Why?
?? Most **stomata** are on the **lower surface**, and water vapour diffuses out through the stomata. The upper surface has a **waxy layer** that stops water loss.
:::

## 4.3 Excretion in humans
:::q 4.3 · Q1 · What is another name for the excretory system?
?? The **renal system**.
:::
:::q 4.3 · Q2 · Carbon dioxide is a waste substance humans excrete. How is it excreted?
?? It's made in **respiration**, carried by the **blood** to the **lungs**, and **breathed out**.
:::
:::q 4.3 · Q3 · Suggest why plants do not produce urea that they have to excrete.
?? Plants **make their own proteins** only as much as they need, from nitrate absorbed from the soil, so they don't take in **excess protein** to break down. (Animals eat more protein than they need, and the liver turns the extra into urea.)
:::
:::q Kidney worksheet · 1 · Label the urinary system (bladder, kidney, renal vein, renal artery, ureter, urethra).
?? **Kidneys** (bean-shaped, top) · **renal artery** and **renal vein** (blood vessels to/from each kidney) · **ureters** (tubes down to the bladder) · **bladder** (bottom) · **urethra** (tube from the bladder to the outside).
:::
:::q Kidney worksheet · 2 · Match each part to its function.
?? **Bladder** – stores urine · **Kidney** – regulates water content and filters the blood · **Renal vein** – carries deoxygenated blood away from the kidneys · **Renal artery** – carries oxygenated blood to the kidneys · **Ureter** – carries urine from the kidneys to the bladder · **Urethra** – carries urine to the outside of the body.
:::

?? **kidneys** · **renal** · **urea** · **water** · **urine**.
:::
:::q Workbook p. 78 · Ex 4.3 Q2–3 · Complete the diagram of the excretory system; label four other structures.
?? Besides the labelled right kidney, artery and vein: **left kidney**, **ureters**, **bladder**, **urethra**.

## 4.4 Keeping a fetus healthy (teacher's answers, LB p. 161 Q1–3)
:::q 4.4 · LB p. 161 · Q1 · List three substances that move from the mother's blood into the fetus's blood.
?? **Oxygen, nutrients (e.g. glucose), vitamins, minerals, water** (any three).
:::
:::q 4.4 · LB p. 161 · Q2 · List two substances that the fetus excretes, which pass into the mother's blood.
?? **Carbon dioxide and urea.**
:::
:::q 4.4 · LB p. 161 · Q3 · Explain why a pregnant woman needs plenty of vitamins A, C and D.
?? **A** — good **vision** for mother and fetus, and **white blood cells** to fight pathogens. **C** — strong **skin and blood vessels** for both. **D** — strong **bones and teeth** for mother and fetus.
:::
