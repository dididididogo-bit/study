# Transpiration

**Key words:** transpiration · wilted

## How water moves through leaves
- Water arrives in the leaf through the **xylem vessels** and moves into the **leaf cells**.
- Cells with chloroplasts use a little of the water for **photosynthesis**, but most of the water does not stay in the cell.
- The liquid water soaks into the **cellulose cell wall** and **evaporates** (changes to water vapour). The water vapour **diffuses** into the **air spaces** between the cells.
- The air spaces connect with the outside air through tiny holes on the **underside** of the leaf — the **stomata**. Water vapour diffuses out through them into the air.

:::key
**Transpiration** = the loss of water vapour from leaves (mostly through the stomata).
:::

**Leaf layers (top to bottom):** upper epidermis → **palisade layer** → **spongy layer** (with air spaces and xylem vessel) → lower epidermis with **stomata**.

## Why plants need water
| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Support** | Plant cells contain a lot of water, especially in their **vacuoles**. A cell full of water is **strong and firm**; cells press on each other and keep the plant upright. Without enough water cells become soft and floppy and the plant collapses — it has **wilted**. |
| **Transport** | Water moving up the xylem carries **dissolved mineral salts** from the roots to all parts of the plant. |
| **Cooling** | When water **evaporates** it takes heat away, cooling the leaf cells — important in very hot places. |
| **Photosynthesis** | Water is a **reactant**: plants change water and carbon dioxide into glucose and oxygen. |
