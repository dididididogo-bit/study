# Unit 1 Views and voices: Use of English

## Verb + -ing (gerund)
After some verbs and prepositions we use the **-ing form**:
- *I don't like **swimming** in the sea.* · *I don't mind **having** a pizza.*
- *I'm going to stop **eating** chocolate every day.* · *Thank you **for coming** to see me.*
- Common ones: **like, don't mind, stop, enjoy, love, hate, thank you for**.

## Present simple vs present continuous
- **Present simple** → habits, routines, facts, **stative verbs** (*agree, know, like, want*): *I **agree** with you.* *I **have** swimming practice every Wednesday.* *I **don't take** sugar.*
- **Present continuous** → happening **now** or a **future arrangement**: *He**'s talking** to Mr Lin.* *Are we **going** on a school trip?*

:::warn
**agree** is a stative verb → never *I'm agreeing*. Routines (*every Wednesday*, *I don't take sugar*) → present simple.
:::

## Present simple passive
**am / is / are + past participle** (by + agent is often left out).
- *People tell me that I'm loud.* → *I **am told** that I'm a bit loud sometimes.*
- *My parents expect me to tidy my room.* → *I **am expected** to tidy my room every Saturday.*

## Present continuous passive (book p. 23)
**am / is / are + being + past participle**
- Used when we **don't know who is doing something, or it isn't important**: the focus is on the thing/person the action happens to.
- *(The audience need to feel that) they **are being entertained**.*
- *Why can't we go into the drama studio? → Because it**'s being repainted**.*
- *A new drama studio **is being built**.*

## Giving a good presentation (1.6 From ideas into words)
**Paragraph headings** for the rules: Make it visual · Timing is important · Practice makes perfect · Get it right · Don't put too much text on a slide · Involve your audience right from the start · Don't be nervous about the audience interrupting you · Plan your presentation carefully.

| Rule | Heading |
|---|---|
| a) Mistakes give a bad impression: check spelling, punctuation and grammar. | Get it right |
| b) Rehearse carefully; speak clearly and slowly, like actors in a theatre. | Practice makes perfect |
| c) After the title slide, show a slide with text/picture and ask a question. | Involve your audience right from the start |
| d) The audience must read your slides easily: usually four lines per slide is enough. | Don't put too much text on a slide |
| e) "A picture is worth a thousand words." | Make it visual |
| f) Have a clear idea of the structure before deciding text and pictures. | Plan your presentation carefully |
| g) Time your presentation and note when each slide should appear. | Timing is important |
| h) The audience is more engaged if they can comment and ask questions. | Don't be nervous about the audience interrupting you |

:::tip
**Making suggestions:** *We could … · Let's … · Why don't we …? · Shall we …?*
:::

## Project 2: a ten-minute presentation on teenage life (13-10-26)
Work in groups. Plan it in five sections:
1. **Stereotypes**: positive or negative?
2. **The teenage brain**: is it different?
3. **Life at school**: in the classroom / outside the classroom
4. **Role models**: who are they? are they important?
5. **Conclusion and questions**

Steps: plan how you'll work (*Shall we all plan…? Could you make notes…? Let's write the script together…*) → write a draft and prepare slides → check slides (not too much text? grammar, spelling, punctuation?) → rehearse (script or notes? timing: 10 minutes?) → make changes → give the presentation → reflect: *what have you learned?*
