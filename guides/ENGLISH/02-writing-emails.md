# Writing emails: choose the right style

## The task (exam-style)
A friend is going to stay in your house while you are on holiday. Write an email to your friend:
- tell them **where important things are**;
- **ask them to do something** for you;
- **suggest things to do** in the area.

Write **100–150 words**. Marks: **7 for content**, **6 for style and accuracy**.

## Two example emails (book p. 23, 0.2)
- **Email A (informal):** *Dear Helen, … Love, Alice*: a friend writing to a friend.
- **Email B (formal):** *Dear Mr Davis, …* (about **guitar lessons**) *… Kind regards, Harry Miller*.

## Formal vs informal
| | Formal | Informal |
|---|---|---|
| **Greeting** | Dear Mr / Mrs / Ms + surname; Dear Sir or Madam | Dear + first name; Hi …; Hello … |
| **Opening** | I am writing to … / I would like to … | Thanks for your email! / How are you? / Guess what! |
| **Vocabulary** | more formal words (*receive, require, inform, however*) | everyday words, phrasal verbs (*get, need, tell, but*) |
| **Verb forms** | **full forms** (*I am, I would, do not*) | **contractions** (*I'm, I'd, don't*) |
| **Closing remarks** | I look forward to hearing from you. / Thank you for your help. | Write soon! / Can't wait to see you! / Take care. |
| **Ending** | Kind regards, / Yours sincerely, (+ full name) | Love, / Best wishes, / See you soon, (+ first name) |

:::tip
**Checklist for an informal email to a friend:** greeting with first name → friendly opening → paragraph for each point of the task (where things are · what to do · suggestions) → closing remark → *Love / Best wishes* + your name. Use contractions and **count your words** (100–150).
:::
