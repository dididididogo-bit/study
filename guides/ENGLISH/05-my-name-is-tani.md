# 1.9 Autobiography: *My Name is Tani*

*My Name is Tani* is by **Tanitoluwa Adewumi**. Subtitle: *"The amazing true story of one boy's journey from refugee to chess champion"*.

## Background
- Tani's family left their home in **Abuja, the capital of Nigeria**, and sought **asylum** in America.
- They first stayed with relatives in **Dallas**, then moved to **New York** and lived in a **shelter for homeless people** in Manhattan (Tani calls it **"the Hotel"**).
- His **father** had been a successful businessman in Nigeria; in America he worked as a **taxi driver** and washing dishes in a restaurant kitchen. His **mother**, an accountant, worked as a **cleaner**.
- At his elementary school a teacher, **Shawn Martinez**, introduced Tani to **chess**. He learned quickly and at **eight** won the **New York State Championship** for third-grade children.
- Tani's coaches raised money through **crowdfunding** to give the family a place to live.

## Extract 1: What Austin and Tani like about their parents
- Walking to the basketball court, Tani (8) and his older brother **Austin** talk about Mum and Dad.
- **Dad:** hardworking, wants the best for them, always reminding them to focus, pay attention and become great people; Austin also likes "his suits" (funny, not rude: Dad really has a lot of suits).
- **Mum:** "the best at teaching moral lessons"; always guiding them away from bad things (Austin remembered her stories on a school trip when kids were messing around).
- Tani's own answer (in his head): *I like that she **cooks good food** · I like that she **corrects me** · Most of all, I like that she **helps me with my chess**.* She never gets angry when he loses; she asks *"Do you think you can do better?"*, takes him to tournaments, cooks, takes Austin to basketball, and has her own job too.

:::note
**Vocabulary from context**
- *that was funny, but not in a rude way*: amusing without being disrespectful.
- *some kids were messing around*: behaving badly / being silly.
- *but I nodded anyway*: agreed (moved his head) even though he already knew.
- *I'm grateful for that*: thankful.
- *she doesn't … make a big deal out of it*: she doesn't treat it as something very serious.
:::

## Extract 2: Coach Shawn's lesson
- Coach Shawn says knowing the moves is **not enough**: the concept of the game is deeper, you must learn to **make good decisions**.
- He asks *"Raise your hand if you know how to cross the street"*: crossing is simple, **but** if you forget to look both ways *"you'd be hit by a car, and you might die"*.
- **Conclusion:** even a simple thing can have really bad **consequences**; in chess, a quick move without thinking can get you into trouble. *"You've got to think the consequences through. You've got to think deep."*
- Tani: *"That's when I knew that I really wanted to be a chess player."*
